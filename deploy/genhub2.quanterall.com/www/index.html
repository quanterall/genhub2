Virus classification for genetic labs, on the lab's own nodes

Upload a genome or a read set. GenHub2 names the virus, lists its mutations and lineage, places it in a tree and links it to public knowledge. The data stays at the lab: labs share counts, not sequences, and every result carries the record of how it was made.

Open the GenHub2 site Source on GitHub

The site at /app/ is Quanterall's own GenHub2 site. It holds public genomes only, and its accounts are by invitation. It is not for patients' data: a lab runs GenHub2 on its own nodes.

What it does

Classification

Species, genus and family with a confidence, from MinHash against every complete viral genome in NCBI RefSeq (Mash). For weak calls, a protein search against all RefSeq viral proteins (DIAMOND).

Mutations and lineages

Substitutions, insertions and deletions against the reference, and amino-acid changes. Lineages and clades from Nextclade's datasets: SARS-CoV-2, influenza, RSV A and B, mpox, Ebola, measles, dengue, hMPV, rubella, mumps and West Nile.

Reads to genome

Human reads removed at the lab (Hostile or minimap2), primers trimmed (iVar), mixed samples resolved (Kraken 2 and Bracken), and the sample's consensus genome.

Trees

Neighbour-joining with bootstrap support, FastTree and VeryFastTree; a new genome placed in the site's SARS-CoV-2 tree in seconds (UShER). Interactive in the page; SVG and Newick to download.

Monitoring

Counts by week, region, lineage and mutation. Alerts for a lineage not seen before and for a rising mutation, from the lab's own counts and from counts exchanged with other labs, without sequences or sample names.

Sharing and discovery

Datasets approved by the lab's admin, with consent codes (GA4GH DUO), read through GA4GH DRS, anonymised. Researchers search them by mutation or lineage through Beacon v2 and get yes or no.

Protein structures

A genome's amino-acid changes, drawn on the virus's protein structures in the TechUPGRADE Digital Twin Platform's view, opened from the result with a signed, short-lived hand-off. Information for experts, not a diagnosis. The view says which datasets have been independently reviewed; none has been yet.

For a lab's systems

An HTTP API and live events on a WebSocket and on MQTT (EMQX): a sequencer's integration can start an analysis by publishing one message. Results as CSV, PDF and JSON.

How a lab runs it

  1. The lab's own nodes. GenHub2 runs on the lab's machines (NVIDIA DGX Spark in our tests). The lab's raw data and the human reads never leave them.
  2. PQCF underneath. Each analysis is a task described as plain data, run on this node, on the lab's other nodes or on a federation peer, with a provenance record of every result (PQCF).
  3. The FVLLMP federation. Labs' nodes meet through mutual-TLS gates, with no central point. A lab can send work that carries no patient data to a peer, and exchange monitoring counts. Task images are handed over privately, never through a public registry.

Security and privacy

  • Two factors: a password and a one-time code from an authenticator app (TOTP, RFC 6238). Passwords are kept only as PBKDF2-SHA256 hashes, 600,000 iterations (RFC 8018).
  • Roles: admin, analyst and researcher; every endpoint checks the session's role.
  • An audit log that shows tampering: logins, uploads, analyses, views, exports and refusals, each record carrying the hash of the one before.
  • Encryption at rest: a lab's site refuses to start unless its data is on an encrypted volume. This public site holds only public genomes and runs with that check as a warning.
  • Sharing on purpose only: nothing leaves a lab unless its admin approves a dataset, with consent codes; between labs, monitoring travels as counts.
